A closer look at the proposed citizenship changes.
Version 0.10 · 29 September 2026

Tests that do not exist yet

Asking applicants to show some English or Irish, and a basic knowledge of how the State works, is a reasonable idea in itself. The difficulty is that the Scheme makes both tests a condition of citizenship before there is a test to sit. [1]

Nothing about the tests has been decided

In September 2026 the Minister confirmed that the language standard, the testing method and the exemptions were still undecided. The civics content and pass mark were not settled either. All of this is left to regulations made after the Bill is passed. [2]

The Scheme allows the language test in English, Irish or Irish Sign Language and permits waivers. It says nothing about fees, where tests would be held, preparation material, how the Irish and Irish Sign Language options would be assessed, or when the service would start. [1]

The scale of the task

Ireland received 41,511 naturalisation applications in 2025. At two tests per person, with 20% extra sittings for retakes, that is about 99,627 sittings a year. [3]

This is a planning estimate: 200 operating days a year and one room per centre. The middle case needs 13 centres; if 30% of applicants were exempt from both tests, it would need nine. The application total includes children, some of whom may be exempt, so the real figure depends on exemption rules that do not exist yet. Calculation and assumptions.

Eligibility would depend on a test place

If the tests begin before the service can cope, people who meet every other condition would be waiting for an appointment rather than for a decision on their case. The Scheme contains no timetable or capacity commitment that rules this out. [1]
