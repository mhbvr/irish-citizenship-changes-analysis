A closer look at the proposed citizenship changes.
Version 0.12 · 29 September 2026, 17:59 IST

Wider revocation powers, no stated gap

Losing citizenship can end a person’s right to live in Ireland and affects their whole family. The revocation procedure was rebuilt only recently, and the Scheme would widen it again.

What the figures show

Minister’s answer, 16 September 2026
Measure Number
Revocations in the preceding ten years 8
Of those, since the revised procedure began on 7 April 2025 2
Fraud-related grounds 7
Loyalty/residence grounds 1

The Department already reviews terrorism, fraud, organised crime and serious-crime cases for possible revocation. Existing grounds include fraud and an overt act showing failure of loyalty to the State. [1] [2]

What Head 10 adds

The Scheme adds public policy, public order and national security as grounds. It cites a changed security environment, but gives no concrete case showing why existing grounds are insufficient. Eight cases alone cannot establish that a new power is unnecessary; the missing evidence is the specific legal gap. [3]

It also removes consultation with the chairperson of the Committee of Inquiry, a retired judge, when the Minister makes procedural regulations. This is not a requirement to consult the chair before initiating an individual case. Notice, representations and the right to request an inquiry remain. [3] [2]

Concerns raised in 2024 still apply

The 2024 changes followed a Supreme Court finding against the old procedure. In July 2024, the Irish Human Rights and Equality Commission called for more scrutiny. Its concerns included the initiation threshold, the inquiry’s independence and restrictions when national-security material is involved. That was its assessment of the 2024 amendments. [1] [4]

No case has been published that the existing grounds could not deal with. “Public policy” is not defined; it sits in the text beside national security without any explanation of what it adds. [3]
