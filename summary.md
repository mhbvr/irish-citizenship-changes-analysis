# The proposed citizenship changes: our position in brief

**Version 0.18 · 26 October 2026** · A summary of the
[full analysis](https://mrphs.cc/position/) of the General Scheme of the Irish Nationality
and Citizenship (Amendment) Bill 2026, published on 21 September 2026
([Scheme, PDF](https://assets.gov.ie/static/documents/cadca21c/General_Scheme_of_the_Irish_Nationality_and_Citizenship_Amendment_Bill_2026.pdf)).
The analysis is also available [as a PDF](citizenship-analysis.pdf).

## Position

The Scheme would make citizenship slower and much less predictable for the people Ireland
has deliberately recruited: nurses, doctors, carers, engineers and researchers. It does
this without saying what problem it solves. Most of what would decide eligibility is
left to future regulations. It has no transition rule and no start date. In our view it
should not go ahead until its case is made with Irish evidence and for Irish needs.

The Irish Human Rights and Equality Commission
([IHREC submission](https://www.ihrec.ie/downloads/2026.10.09-IHREC-Submission-General-Scheme-INCA-Bill-2026-Final-3.pdf))
and the Bar of Ireland
([Bar submission](https://www.lawlibrary.ie/app/uploads/securepdfs/2026/10/TBOI-Submission-Nationality-Citizenship-Amendment-Bill.pdf))
reach many of the same conclusions in their pre-legislative scrutiny submissions.

## What the Scheme proposes

- **Residence:** the ordinary route rises from five qualifying years to eight, with the
  last two continuous. For spouses and civil partners of Irish citizens, both the
  marriage or partnership and the residence rise from three years to five.
- **New conditions:** a minimum income, restrictions on prescribed welfare and housing
  supports with a two-year lookback, a language test, a civics test, and a bar for
  scheduled immigration offences with no time limit.
- **Fewer years count:** time on temporary protection and on some other permissions stops
  counting, and the Minister can exclude more permissions by regulation.
- **Good character:** the Minister could no longer waive it in any circumstances.
- **Revocation:** public policy, public order and national security become grounds for
  revoking citizenship. The Committee of Inquiry's chair loses the right to be consulted
  on the inquiry's procedures.

## Our main concerns

**1. It falls on the people Ireland recruited.**
In 2025, 29,919 certificates were issued. 64.6% went to adults on the ordinary route, and
one in three went to a child or to the spouse of an Irish citizen. The largest
nationalities broadly match the countries Ireland recruited workers from through its own
permit system (a comparison of nationalities, not of individual records). Nearly half of practising nurses and midwives trained outside the EU, and 43.4% of
doctors qualified outside Ireland. Non-Irish citizens account for 47% of the rise in
employment between 2021 and 2026, and on ESRI's estimates immigrants pay more into the
public finances than they take out. Changing citizenship does not change who arrives;
it changes the prospects of people already here.
→ [Chapter 1](https://mrphs.cc/position/applicants.html) ·
[certificate tables](https://data.oireachtas.ie/ie/oireachtas/debates/questions/supportingDocumentation/2026-01-22_pq-482-486a-22-01-2026_en.docx) ·
[NMBI](https://www.nmbi.ie/NMBI/media/NMBI/NMBI-State-of-The-Register-2025.pdf?ext=.pdf#page=12) ·
[Medical Council](https://www.medicalcouncil.ie/news-and-publications/publications/medical-workforce-intelligence-report-2025.pdf) ·
[CSO QLF59](https://data.cso.ie/table/QLF59) ·
[ESRI](https://www.esri.ie/system/files/publications/SUSTAT141.pdf)

**2. Eight years, with no stated reason and no transition.**
The Scheme's only reason is a "proportionate timeline". It doesn't say why eight years is
proportionate and five is not. The EU has no common rule, and Germany, France, Belgium,
the Netherlands and others still allow five. Nothing protects people part-way through
the five years, and the start date is left to ministerial order. The Minister of State has
also said the change applies "from enactment". A PhD graduate who stays in Ireland would
wait twelve years rather than nine.
→ [Chapter 2](https://mrphs.cc/position/residence.html) ·
[PQ 894](https://www.oireachtas.ie/en/debates/question/2026-09-16/894/) ·
[European Parliament brief](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/BRIE/2025/769502/EPRS_BRI(2025)769502_EN.pdf)

**3. The substance is left to regulations.**
The income figure, the welfare and housing supports that count against an applicant, the
language level, the civics syllabus, pass marks, exemptions and further excluded
permissions are all left to regulations. In September 2026 the Minister of State confirmed
that the language standard, the testing method, the exemptions and the civics content
had not been decided, and that the income details are still to be developed. Nobody
can yet check whether they would qualify.
The Bar of Ireland warns that delegating this much may breach Article 15.2.1° of the
Constitution. The tests would also need capacity: on the analysis's planning
assumptions, 41,511 applications a year would mean about 100,000 test sittings.
→ [Chapter 3](https://mrphs.cc/position/tests.html) ·
[PQ 2397](https://www.oireachtas.ie/en/debates/question/2026-09-07/2397/)

**4. An income test that judges people by their bank balance.**
The test asks whether a long-term resident is an "unreasonable burden on the State". The
people most likely to fall below an income line at a given moment are carers, disabled
people and mothers on leave or working part-time. Women are 61% of unpaid carers, and
their median earnings are about €8,500 below men's. A job loss while waiting could end
an application. The premise is also weak: non-EU migrants in Ireland paid a net €1,047 a
year per person, more than people born in Ireland (€940). IHREC warns the rules could
become indirectly discriminatory.
→ [Chapter 4](https://mrphs.cc/position/income.html) ·
[CSO earnings](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-eaads/earningsanalysisusingadministrativedatasources2024/annualearnings/) ·
[CSO carers](https://www.cso.ie/en/releasesandpublications/ep/p-cpp4/censusofpopulation2022profile4-disabilityhealthandcarers/carers/)

**5. Offences and good character without proportion.**
Schedule 1 includes paperwork duties, such as notifying a change of address within 48
hours. A listed offence would bar citizenship permanently, while ordinary theft would
still be judged case by case under the good-character test. Head 8 would also remove the
Minister's power to waive good character, including the current waiver for children aged
14 to 17, and gives no reason specific to children. The Bar of Ireland calls the offence
condition categorical and lacking proportionality. IHREC asks whether the good-character
change complies with the rights of the child.
→ [Chapter 5](https://mrphs.cc/position/proportionality.html)

**6. Wider revocation powers with no stated gap.**
Eight people lost citizenship in ten years: seven for fraud and one on loyalty or
residence grounds. The Scheme adds public policy, public order and national security as
grounds without defining any of them. It doesn't show a case the existing grounds could
not deal with. Combined with the existing power to withhold reasons on security grounds,
a person could lose citizenship without knowing the case against them. The Bar of Ireland
argues this shifts the burden of proof onto the citizen. IHREC raises legal certainty, EU
law and the Supreme Court's Damache judgment, and expects litigation.
→ [Chapter 6](https://mrphs.cc/position/revocation.html) ·
[PQ 883](https://www.oireachtas.ie/en/debates/question/2026-09-16/883/)

**7. Families in Northern Ireland are overlooked.**
The spouse route counts residence anywhere on the island, but the explanatory note
speaks of residence "in the state". The tests are framed around the State. An unmarried
partner of an Irish citizen in Northern Ireland can't count any residence, although the
same partner could qualify for British citizenship. This point comes from the Bar of
Ireland's submission.
→ [Chapter 2, Northern Ireland](https://mrphs.cc/position/residence.html#northern-ireland) ·
[Chapter 3](https://mrphs.cc/position/tests.html#northern-ireland)

## What a sound proposal would need

- **Evidence:** an Irish reason for eight years, and an estimate of who the income and
  test conditions would exclude, and of the effect on recruitment and retention in
  health, care and technology.
- **Transition:** protection for people already building up residence, and a clear
  start date.
- **Primary legislation:** clear criteria in the Bill itself for income, supports and
  tests, with regulations approved by the Oireachtas.
- **Proportion:** a time limit and a seriousness threshold for the offence bar, and a
  waiver of good character for exceptional cases, especially for children.
- **Revocation:** defined grounds, evidence of a real legal gap, and safeguards that
  meet the Damache standard.
- **All-island rules:** explicit rules for applicants and families in Northern Ireland.
