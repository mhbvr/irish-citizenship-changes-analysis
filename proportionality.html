A closer look at the proposed citizenship changes.
Version 0.11 · 29 September 2026, 13:06 IST

A permanent bar for a paperwork offence?

The proposed Schedule is not confined to identity fraud or illegal entry. It also reaches administrative duties. Under Heads 5 and 6, committing a listed offence would create a separate citizenship bar with no time limit in the wording. [1]

Three concrete examples

Existing duties included through Schedule 1
Duty Legal provision
Notify a new home address within 48 hours if it was not notified in advance. Immigration Act 2004, sections 9(2)(c) and 9(8). [2]
Notify the current address when absent from the registered residence for more than one month. Sections 9(2)(d) and 9(8). [2]
Produce a passport or equivalent document and, where registered, a registration certificate on a lawful demand. Section 12. A reasonable-cause defence applies. [3]

These examples concern people to whom the duties apply. They are not a claim that every late update or missing document automatically proves an offence. They show the range of conduct covered by the proposed bar.

Good character does not demand perfection

In Hussain v Minister for Justice [2011] IEHC 171, paragraph 15, Hogan J. said the standard was “reasonable standards of civic responsibility as gauged by reference to contemporary values”. Applicants could not be held to behaviour unrealistically higher than that expected of Irish people. [4]

The judgment also permits consideration of serious wrongdoing without a conviction, provided fair procedures are followed. The issue is proper evidence and an individual assessment, not an entitlement to ignore misconduct. [4]

The bar is not ordered by seriousness

A scheduled registration offence receives an express bar with no expiry, while a non-scheduled offence such as ordinary theft remains subject to the good-character assessment. That does not guarantee citizenship after theft; it shows that the Schedule’s treatment is not simply ordered by seriousness. [1] [5]

The Scheme says nothing about how an offence would be proved, how far back it can reach, or how an old breach could be reviewed. As written, an address change notified late and later corrected would carry the same permanent consequence as deliberate fraud. [1]
