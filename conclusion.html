Conclusion

Each chapter looks at one part of the Scheme. Read together, they describe a proposal that falls on people Ireland has already chosen to recruit, and leaves them unable to plan.

Who carries the change

Most people who become Irish citizens came here to work: nurses, care workers, doctors and IT specialists, many on employment permits for jobs the State listed as hard to fill. In 2025 one certificate in three went to a child or to the spouse or civil partner of an Irish citizen. These are settled families, not new arrivals. [1] [2]

They are also people Ireland depends on. Nearly half of the nurses and midwives in practice trained outside the EU, and non-Irish citizens filled 47% of the jobs created between 2021 and 2026. On the best available estimates, immigrants in Ireland pay more into the public finances than they take out. [3] [4] [5]

Too much is left open

The Scheme fixes the new residence periods but leaves most of what decides eligibility to regulations that do not yet exist: the income figure, the welfare payments and housing supports that would count against an applicant, the language level, the civics syllabus, the pass marks, the exemptions and any further permissions that would stop counting as residence. The Minister confirmed in September 2026 that the tests had not been designed. [6] [7]

No start date is set; each part would begin on a day chosen by ministerial order. The Scheme gives no reason for eight years rather than five, publishes no estimate of how many people the income test would exclude, and contains no assessment of the effect on recruitment or retention. [6]

Plans already made

A person who began uninterrupted qualifying residence in January 2022 reaches five years in January 2027 and eight in January 2030. If the new rule applies before they can submit, their application moves three years later, before processing even begins. The Scheme protects applications already lodged from its new residence exclusions, but nobody who is still working towards eligibility. [8] [6]

The same gap runs through the other conditions. The income lookback reaches back two years into a period when the rules were unknown, and the income and support conditions continue until a decision, so a job loss or illness while waiting could end an application. [6]

Families and children

Children received 6,949 certificates in 2025. For children born after the change, Head 4 would alter which parental residence counts towards citizenship at birth, including excluding time on temporary protection and on any further permissions the Minister excludes by regulation. [1] [6]

What Ireland stands to lose

Ireland competes for skilled workers and researchers with other countries. Germany, France, the Netherlands and Belgium ask for five years. A PhD graduate who stays in Ireland would wait twelve years under the proposal, and until then may need a Schengen visa for each conference abroad. [9] [10] [11]

The Government’s own talent strategy aims to attract international researchers and PhD students. [12] The Scheme pulls the other way, with a longer route, conditions nobody can yet check and no date. That is the main cost of the proposal as published: a route to citizenship that the people Ireland most wants to keep can no longer plan around.
